## Model scope

### Snell’s Law and Total Internal Reflection

Trace a ray across a dielectric boundary and locate the onset of total internal reflection.

**Reference example:** Glass to air at 30° gives θ₂=48.5904°; the critical angle is 41.8103°.

**Limits:** Lossless isotropic media, planar interface and monochromatic geometric optics. A null transmitted angle denotes an evanescent field, not a propagating ray.

### Thin Lens and Ray Diagram

Construct principal rays for a converging or diverging thin lens, including virtual and infinite images.

**Reference example:** f=10 cm and u=30 cm give v=15 cm and m=−0.5.

**Limits:** Paraxial thin lens in air. u>0 for a real object; f>0 converging; v>0 real image. At u=f the image is at infinity and is reported as null.

### Double-Slit Interference and Diffraction Grating

Resolve the narrow interference peaks inside the single-slit diffraction envelope.

**Reference example:** λ=550 nm, d=20 µm and L=1 m give fringe spacing 27.5 mm.

**Limits:** Fraunhofer scalar diffraction, equal coherent slits and uniform illumination. Intensity is normalized to the central maximum. Screen geometry uses θ=atan(x/L).

### Fresnel Reflection and Brewster Angle

Compare the two polarization-dependent reflectances and the Brewster minimum.

**Reference example:** Air to n=1.5 glass: θB=56.3099°, Rs(0)=Rp(0)=0.04.

**Limits:** Nonmagnetic lossless dielectrics, no coatings or absorption. TIR power reflectance is unity; phase shifts and evanescent penetration are outside this model.

### Electric Field and Potential of Point Charges

Superpose electric fields and potentials from two independently signed point charges.

**Reference example:** ±1 nC at x=±0.5 m give V=0 on x=0; at y=0.5 m the field points toward the negative charge.

**Limits:** Static point charges in vacuum. A 0.025 m exclusion radius removes singular charge locations; field arrows are direction-normalized for visibility.

### Biot–Savart Magnetic Field of Current Loops

Evaluate the Biot–Savart integral on the axis of a circular current loop.

**Reference example:** Ten turns, I=5 A and R=0.1 m give B(0)=0.314159 mT.

**Limits:** Thin coincident circular turns in free space; on-axis field only. No ferromagnetic core, finite wire radius or mutual-inductance dynamics.

### Faraday Induction and Lenz’s Law

Rotate a coil and compare linked flux with its negative time derivative.

**Reference example:** N=20, B=0.2 T, A=0.01 m² and f=2 Hz give Epk=0.502655 V.

**Limits:** Prescribed rotation in a uniform static field; resistive load only. Flux and voltage traces carry different units as labeled. Back torque, inductance and rotation dynamics are omitted.

### Transmission-Line Reflection and VSWR

Superpose incident and reflected waves on a lossless line with a complex load.

**Reference example:** Z₀=50 Ω and ZL=100 Ω give Γ=1/3 and VSWR=2.

**Limits:** Lossless uniform line and passive load. Purely reactive or shorted loads have infinite VSWR, shown as null. Distance is measured toward the source from the load.

### Rectangular Waveguide Modes and Cutoff

Select rectangular-guide TE/TM indices and compare propagating and evanescent regimes.

**Reference example:** WR-90 TE10 has fc≈6.557 GHz; at 10 GHz its guide wavelength is about 39.7 mm.

**Limits:** Perfectly conducting air-filled guide; no conductor/dielectric loss. Below cutoff guide wavelength and group speed are null. The diagram is a normalized transverse field pattern, not full-vector FEM.

### Fourier Series Synthesis and Gibbs Phenomenon

Build a discontinuous or continuous waveform from its actual Fourier coefficients.

**Reference example:** A square wave’s first term is (4/π)sin(2πt); its value at the jump is the midpoint, zero.

**Limits:** Unit-period targets; coefficients use symmetric midpoint values at discontinuities. The overshoot metric is relative to amplitude one, not jump height.

### FFT Spectrum, Leakage and Windowing

Compute a radix-2 FFT of a sampled sinusoid and observe leakage and coherent-gain correction.

**Reference example:** For fs=256 Hz and N=256, bins are 1 Hz apart. A 17 Hz rectangular-window tone occupies one positive-frequency bin.

**Limits:** One real unit-amplitude tone, no additive noise, no interpolation. Windows are symmetric N−1 forms; ENBW=NΣw²/(Σw)².

### Laplace Pole–Zero and Frequency Response

Move a conjugate pole pair and a real zero, and evaluate H(jω) directly.

**Reference example:** Poles −1±2j and zero −3 give DC gain 3/5 at K=1.

**Limits:** Continuous-time proper rational SISO model. dB floors at −240 dB avoid log zero; phase is principal angle. No hidden pole-zero cancellation stability inference.

### FIR Filter Design by the Window Method

Form and normalize a finite windowed-sinc low-pass impulse response, then evaluate its discrete frequency response.

**Reference example:** A symmetric 31-tap filter has group delay (31−1)/2=15 samples.

**Limits:** Odd-length symmetric type-I low-pass FIR. DC normalization is applied after windowing; cutoff is the ideal discontinuity and is approximately −6 dB, not the IIR −3 dB definition.

### IIR Butterworth and the Bilinear Transform

Design an actual second-order digital Butterworth biquad with cutoff prewarping.

**Reference example:** At the specified digital cutoff the design has |H|=1/√2 and −3.0103 dB.

**Limits:** Fixed second order low-pass, double precision. The comparison is an unwarped analog Butterworth magnitude evaluated at digital frequency, not another digital realization.

### Z-Transform Pole–Zero and Stability

Relate causal z-plane poles to an explicitly iterated impulse response.

**Reference example:** A radius below one produces a decaying envelope; at radius one oscillations persist.

**Limits:** Causal initial-rest second-order filter; exact cancellations are not used to reclassify internal pole stability. Principal frequency angle is 0…π.

### Correlation and Time-Delay Estimation

Shift a nonperiodic Gaussian pulse and recover signed delay by normalized cross-correlation.

**Reference example:** For y[n]=x[n−12] without noise, the correlation peak is at lag +12.

**Limits:** Finite noncircular records; candidate lags −40…40. Noise is a reproducible two-tone interference, not random white noise; overlap is normalized independently for each lag.

### Spectrogram and Time–Frequency Tradeoff

Compute a Hann-window short-time Fourier transform of a linear chirp and inspect its actual heat map.

**Reference example:** At fs=512 Hz, a 64-sample window spans 0.125 s and has 8 Hz bin spacing.

**Limits:** One-second synthetic linear chirp, fs=512 Hz, no zero padding. Plot follows the strongest bin; the image shows log power clipped to a 60 dB range.

### Root Locus of a Third-Order System

Solve all three roots of the unity-feedback characteristic polynomial while sweeping positive loop gain.

**Reference example:** For a=1,b=2 the boundary is K=6, with poles −3 and ±j√2.

**Limits:** Plant G=K/[s(s+a)(s+b)], unity negative feedback, positive gain. Numerically solved roots are shown; no unmodeled delay or saturation.

### Gain and Phase Margin from Loop Bode Plot

Locate gain and phase crossovers by solving the loop response rather than choosing the closest plotted sample.

**Reference example:** For a=1,b=5,K=2, phase crossover is √5 rad/s and gain margin is 20log10(6/2)=9.5424 dB.

**Limits:** Continuous minimum-phase loop, no delay and one gain/phase crossover. Magnitude and phase use different units explicitly labeled; frequency axis uses logarithmically spaced samples.

### State-Space Pole Placement

Place both poles of a controllable double integrator and integrate the closed-loop state equations.

**Reference example:** Desired poles −1 and −2 give K=[2,3] for A=[[0,1],[0,0]], B=[0,1]ᵀ.

**Limits:** Full noiseless state feedback, normalized unit mass and unlimited actuator. RK4 step ≤0.005 s; the model is a regulator around zero, not arbitrary tracking.

### LQR Weight Tuning

Solve the continuous algebraic Riccati equation for a double integrator and inspect cost versus control effort.

**Reference example:** Q=I and R=1 yield K=[1,√3], P11=√3 and J*=√3 for x₀=[1,0].

**Limits:** Continuous-time unit-mass double integrator, positive diagonal Q and positive scalar R. Infinite-horizon unconstrained regulator; not constrained MPC.

### Kalman Filter: Position & Velocity

Estimate position and velocity from noisy position observations.

**Reference example:** The state is [position,velocity], Δt=0.1 s, initial covariance diag(10,10). Change the seed to test robustness.

**Limits:** Constant-velocity model with independent discrete white acceleration, known noise variances and Gaussian assumptions. A single seeded record does not establish filter consistency.

### Luenberger Observer Convergence

Reconstruct double-integrator velocity from measured position using an explicit observer error system.

**Reference example:** Observer poles −2,−3 imply L=[5,6]ᵀ when C=[1,0].

**Limits:** Noiseless measurement convergence study, known input and exact plant. Faster-pole noise tradeoff is discussed but noise is not injected into this deterministic error trajectory.

### Ziegler–Nichols Ultimate-Gain Tuning

Find the exact ultimate oscillation of a three-equal-lag plant and apply the classic P, PI or PID table.

**Reference example:** For G=1/(s+1)³, Ku=8 and Pu=2π/√3≈3.6276 s.

**Limits:** Stable equal-lag process g/(τs+1)³ with unity feedback. Derivative acts on measured output to avoid setpoint kick; no saturation, sensor delay or robustness guarantee.

### Model Predictive Control with a Constrained Horizon

Solve a finite-horizon box-constrained quadratic program at each sample and apply only its first move.

**Reference example:** With |u|≤0.2 and target one from zero, at least five moves are needed to reach the target.

**Limits:** Scalar discrete integrator, perfect state knowledge, 30 applied moves. Projected gradient solves the condensed convex box QP; residual is a numerical convergence indicator. No state constraints, disturbances or terminal stability guarantee.

### PWM Duty, Resolution and Filtering

Quantize a PWM duty command and solve the RC filter exactly over high and low intervals.

**Reference example:** At 3.3 V and 50% duty, a unity-DC-gain RC filter averages 1.65 V.

**Limits:** Ideal switched voltage source and unloaded first-order RC at periodic steady state. The carrier frequency is independently prescribed, so clock budgeting is handled in the timer lab.

### ADC Quantization and SQNR

Quantize a coherent sampled sinusoid with a bipolar mid-rise ADC, including saturation and measured error.

**Reference example:** An ideal 8-bit converter has a full-scale sine SQNR near 49.92 dB.

**Limits:** Ideal bipolar uniform mid-rise quantizer, 512 samples and seven coherent sine cycles. Reported measured ratio includes clipping and DC-offset effects; no analog noise, aperture jitter or nonlinearity.

### Strain-Gauge Wheatstone Bridge

Evaluate exact resistor-divider outputs for quarter, opposed half and full strain-gauge bridges.

**Reference example:** GF=2, ε=1000 µε and 5 V excitation give approximately 2.5 mV in a quarter bridge.

**Limits:** Left lower arm is tensile. Half bridge also has an equal compressive left upper arm; full bridge uses the opposite signs in the right branch. No lead resistance, transverse strain, thermal compensation or amplifier model.

### Thermocouple and RTD Linearization

Compare polynomial thermocouple compensation and Callendar–Van Dusen RTD inversion against a linear calibration.

**Reference example:** A Pt100 at 100°C gives 138.5055 Ω using A=.0039083 and B=−5.775×10⁻⁷.

**Limits:** NIST ITS-90 Type-T polynomial for 0…400°C, EMF in mV; Pt100 CVD positive-temperature branch. Ideal sensors, no self-heating, contact error, lead resistance or measurement noise.

### Switch Debounce and Interrupt Latency

Generate a bouncing contact and accept it only after an uninterrupted stable interval, then apply interrupt latency.

**Reference example:** If the last raw transition is at 6 ms and the stable interval is 8 ms, the accepted edge is at 14 ms.

**Limits:** Deterministic alternating contact transitions at 0, .16B, .32B, .48B, .64B and .8B, final closure at B. Sample resolution 0.05 ms; no analog Schmitt or metastability model.

### UART, SPI and I²C Timing Diagrams

Build protocol-specific bit timelines, including UART framing, SPI clock phase and I²C address/ACK slots.

**Reference example:** UART 8N1 sends 10 bits per data byte; at 100 kbit/s the frame takes 100 µs.

**Limits:** UART 8N1, SPI mode 0 or 3 with active-low CS, I²C single-byte write to an always-ACK receiver. Start/stop timing uses half-bit illustrative slots; no clock stretching, bus capacitance or setup-time compliance analysis.

### Sensor Noise, Averaging and Allan Deviation

Generate seeded white noise plus random-walk drift, then compare block averages and overlapping Allan deviation.

**Reference example:** Pure white noise with σ=0.5 has Allan deviation near 0.5/√m at averaging factor m.

**Limits:** 4096 unit-spaced samples, seeded Gaussian draws. Random walk is synthetic integrated noise, not a calibrated physical sensor. Standard deviations are population statistics of this finite realization.

### Timer Prescaler and Overflow Calculator

Calculate a representable TOP value from clock, divisor and desired interval, including out-of-range targets.

**Reference example:** 16 MHz divided by 64 gives a 4 µs tick; 10 ms needs 2500 ticks, TOP=2499.

Apply the integrated van ’t Hoff relation and inspect how endothermic and exothermic equilibria shift.

**Reference example:** ΔH=0 gives K independent of temperature; K(Tref) equals the specified reference value.

### McCabe–Thiele Distillation Staging

**Limits:** Constant relative volatility, constant molar overflow, saturated-liquid feed q=1, total condenser not counted and partial reboiler counted. Staging capped at 120; null indicates the cap/pinch. No tray efficiencies or energy balances.

**Limits:** Incompressible fixed bed of approximately spherical particles with uniform porosity. No fluidization, gas density change, wall correction or particle-size distribution.

**Reference example:** Maximize 3x+2y subject to x+y≤8, 2x+y≤12, x≤5: optimum (4,4) gives 20.

### Simplex Pivoting Tableau
